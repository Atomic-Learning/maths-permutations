A **permutation** counts the number of ordered ways of selecting $k$ items from a pool of $n$ distinct items, where the order of selection matters.

The number of permutations is written $P(n, k)$ and is given by:

$$P(n, k) = \frac{n!}{(n-k)!}$$

# Worked example

How many ordered arrangements can be made by choosing 2 items from a pool of 3?

$$P(3, 2) = \frac{3!}{(3-2)!} = \frac{6}{1} = 6$$

If the pool is $\{A, B, C\}$, the six arrangements are: $AB$, $AC$, $BA$, $BC$, $CA$, $CB$.

Note that $AB$ and $BA$ are counted separately because **order matters** in a permutation.
